Practical guides for finding support

A calm place to prepare for conversations, compare options and understand the next step.

Before choosing a home-support provider

  • Write down the exact tasks and times help is needed.
  • Ask which tasks the provider does not offer.
  • Request written rates, minimum hours, travel charges and cancellation terms.
  • Ask for proof of appropriate insurance and relevant licences or permits.
  • Ask how staff are screened, trained, supervised and supported.
  • Request references and a written complaint/incident process.
  • For transportation, confirm vehicle type, mobility equipment fit, transfer assistance limits and driver screening.
  • Do not pay a deposit until you understand the agreement and cancellation/refund terms.

Preparing for a Home Care conversation

  • Describe the everyday activities where help is needed, using examples.
  • List current supports and any gaps in the schedule.
  • Ask what assessment information is needed and who will make the decision.
  • Ask what services are publicly provided and what alternatives exist if needs change.
  • Ask whether self/family-managed care is an option and what expenses are permitted.
  • Confirm who to call if a scheduled service is missed or needs change.

Supporting a family caregiver

  • Identify tasks that can be shared with relatives, friends or community services.
  • Ask the care coordinator about respite options and eligibility.
  • Plan backup coverage for illness, bad weather and missed visits.
  • Keep emergency contacts and a simple routine plan accessible to the people who need them.
  • Use 211 Manitoba to find community supports close to home.

211 Manitoba — call 211 or use online support

Planning home accessibility changes

  • Identify the barrier and the activity it affects.
  • Get more than one qualified quote for significant work.
  • Ask about permits, building requirements, warranties and insurance.
  • Confirm grant approval and eligible expenses before signing a contract or starting work.
  • Ask whether a qualified occupational therapist or accessibility professional should assess the proposed change.

Finding local community information

211 Manitoba provides free, confidential navigation to community, health and social services across the province. It is an important existing resource; Independent Living Canada aims to complement rather than duplicate it.
