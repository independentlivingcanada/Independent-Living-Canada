# Community partnership plan — Independent Living Canada

## Purpose
Build referral and information partnerships that help Manitobans find appropriate supports. A partner is not automatically a service provider, and a referral relationship is not an endorsement.

## Priority partner types
1. 211 Manitoba / United Way Winnipeg — explore directory data/referral collaboration and how to avoid duplicating existing navigation services.
2. Manitoba Possible — disability-led expertise, accessibility feedback and potential referral/resource collaboration.
3. Seniors organizations and caregiver groups — lived-experience testing and distribution of practical guides.
4. Municipalities, libraries and community resource centres — trusted local access points and printed resource cards.
5. Regional Health Authorities and community transportation organizations — accurate public-program pathways and local service boundaries.

## First contact: what to ask
- Is there an appropriate contact for community directory or referral partnerships?
- What information can be shared publicly, and how is it kept current?
- What criteria do you use for inclusion, referrals or partner recognition?
- Would you review our Manitoba funding guide for accuracy?
- Would you participate in a short accessibility/user-experience review?
- What should families do when no suitable service is available?

## Safeguards
- Do not imply endorsement, formal partnership or data-sharing until written agreement.
- Do not send personal health or client information by email.
- Obtain consent before any person is referred or their details shared.
- Distinguish community information organizations from paid service providers.
- Publish a date checked and a correction route for resource information.

## Suggested first outreach
Begin with 211 Manitoba and Manitoba Possible, followed by seniors and caregiver organizations. Use each organization's published partnership/contact route and keep a dated outreach log in the restricted staff workflow.

## Lived-experience advisory group
Proposed first cohort: three to five compensated advisors, including older adults, people with disabilities and family caregivers. Invite participants through established community organizations rather than collecting personal details through an untested public form.

Principles:
- Pay an honorarium where funding permits; do not assume unpaid labour.
- Offer accessible meeting formats, plain-language materials, remote/in-person options and support for communication/access needs.
- Obtain informed consent for participation and any use of feedback; do not publish names or stories without separate permission.
- Ask advisors to test navigation, language, service boundaries and trust labels, not to certify providers or provide individual legal/medical advice.
- Record themes and actions without unnecessary personal data.
- Do not describe an advisory group as established until members have accepted and terms of reference are agreed.

No advisors have been recruited or confirmed yet.
